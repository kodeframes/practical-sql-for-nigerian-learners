# Chapter 1: Why SQL Matters and Where the Skill Fits — Explained Solutions

For Level A, the core distinction is management versus language: a database holds organised data, a DBMS manages it, and SQL expresses operations over relational data. Application code may validate input or serve an API; SQL may retrieve orders or update a payment status.

For Level B, reasonable problems include repeated student details, inconsistent names, duplicated invoice information, overwritten historical values, and difficulty separating one student’s balance from another. A relational design could separate students, invoices, and payments and connect them with keys.

For Level C, “best” could mean highest completed-order value, most orders, highest average order value, most recent activity, or strongest payment completion rate. Each definition produces a different metric. The professional step is to make that ambiguity explicit before querying.
