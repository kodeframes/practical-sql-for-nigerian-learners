Identify the grain of `branches`, `orders`, `order_items`, `payments`, and `supplier_products`.

### Level B — Reason

A table `student_payments` contains student name, programme name, programme fee, payment amount, and payment date on every payment row. Identify likely dependencies and propose a better decomposition.

### Level C — Professional Challenge

A reporting team asks to copy `branch_name` into every order because joins are “too slow.” Describe the investigation you would perform before changing the schema, and the consistency problem the copied name would create.

## Hands-on Lab: normalise a deliberately poor order sheet

Start with a fictional flat structure containing columns such as:

```text
order_number, order_date, customer_name, customer_phone,
branch_name, product_1, product_1_qty, product_2, product_2_qty,
payment_1_amount, payment_2_amount
```

Identify three different problems: repeating groups, duplicated entity facts, and mixed grains. Then decompose the structure into at least `customers`, `branches`, `orders`, `order_items`, and `payments`.

For each resulting table, write the grain and primary key. Then identify which foreign keys recreate the relationships that were implicit in the flat sheet.

### Lab verification

Take one original flat-row scenario and show how it would be represented after normalisation. The normalised design may require several rows across several tables, but each fact should now have a clearer home. If changing a customer's phone number still requires rewriting old order rows, the decomposition has not solved the duplication problem.
