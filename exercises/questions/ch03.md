## Hands-on Lab: write the grain before the columns

Choose five canonical tables: `branches`, `customers`, `orders`, `order_items`, and `payments`. Before looking at their column lists, write one sentence describing what one row represents in each table. Then inspect the data dictionary and revise your sentences if necessary.

Next, create a relationship note like this:

```text
branches      1 -> many orders
customers     1 -> many orders, but an order may have no stored customer
orders        1 -> many order_items
orders        1 -> many payments
products      1 -> many order_items
```

The important point is not the arrows themselves. It is the meaning of the optionality. `orders.customer_id` may be `NULL`, so a query that starts from `customers` and joins to `orders` answers a different population question from a query that starts from `orders`.

### Lab verification

Then pick one customer with no orders and explain why that customer is still a valid row in the customer table. This is your first practical test of the difference between entity existence and transaction participation.
